\begin{abstract}
Traditional speculative decoding often incurs unavoidable-looking latency from alternating draft and target executions separated by synchronization. We argue this delay is partly an orchestration artifact. We prototype a heterogeneous, warp-specialized execution model on NVIDIA Blackwell-class hardware that keeps draft messenger warps advancing while validator warps overlap verification and KV-style memory traffic inside a tightly coupled CUDA kernel. Claims are anchored to reproducible GPU microbenchmarks (not production LLM throughput) and juxtaposed honestly against CPU-orchestrated baselines analogous to prevalent frameworks such as HuggingFace \texttt{generate()} pipelines and staged GPU engines like vLLM.
\end{abstract}

From \texttt{results/final\_bench.csv}, the fused pipeline measures 6.334 \(\mu s\) mean latency vs 24.435 \(\mu s\) for the host-synchronized two-kernel baseline, i.e. \(3.86\times\) lower latency for the fused path. Against the serial two-phase stream baseline (8.278 \(\mu s\)), fused remains \(1.31\times\) faster.

\textbf{Disclaimer: } End-to-end speedups versus HuggingFace or vLLM are \emph{not} claimed here unless separately instrumented—the microbench intentionally isolates kernel scheduling effects.
